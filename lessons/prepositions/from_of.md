# Prepositions of Origin & Possession: de, d'

The preposition **de** (and its elided form **d'** before a vowel or silent h) is extremely common in French and is primarily used to express origin or possession.

## 1. Expressing Origin ("from")
- Used to indicate where someone or something comes from.
  - *Example*: Je viens **de** France. (I come from France.)
  - *Example*: Elle arrive **de** la gare. (She arrives from the station.)
  - *Example*: C'est un vin **d'**Italie. (It's a wine from Italy.)

## 2. Expressing Possession ("of" / "'s")
- French does not use an apostrophe-s (`'s`) for possession. Instead, it uses **de**.
  - *Example*: Le livre **de** Pierre. (Pierre's book / The book of Pierre.)
  - *Example*: Le chien **d'**Alice. (Alice's dog.)

## 3. Contractions with Articles
- **de** contracts with definite articles:
  - `de + le` becomes **du**
    - *Example*: Le cahier **du** professeur. (The teacher's notebook.)
  - `de + les` becomes **des**
    - *Example*: Les jouets **des** enfants. (The children's toys.)
  - `de + la` remains **de la**
  - `de + l'` remains **de l'**
