# Present Indicative of Regular -er Verbs (A1)

In French, regular verbs ending in **-er** constitute the largest group of verbs. To conjugate them in the present tense, you remove the **-er** ending from the infinitive to find the stem, and then add the subject-specific endings.

## Conjugation Pattern

For the verb **parler** (to speak), the stem is **parl-**.

| Subject | Conjugated Verb | English Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Je** (I) | Je parl**e** | I speak / I am speaking |
| **Tu** (You singular/informal) | Tu parl**es** | You speak / You are speaking |
| **Il / Elle / On** (He/She/One) | Il/Elle/On parl**e** | He/She/One speaks |
| **Nous** (We) | Nous parl**ons** | We speak / We are speaking |
| **Vous** (You plural/formal) | Vous parl**ez** | You speak / You are speaking |
| **Ils / Elles** (They masc/fem) | Ils/Elles parl**ent** | They speak / They are speaking |

## Special Case: Verbs ending in -ger (like manger)
For verbs ending in **-ger**, keep the **e** in the **Nous** form to keep the pronunciation soft (like a 'j' sound):
*   *Nous mangons* (Incorrect pronunciation)
*   *Nous mang**e**ons* (Correct)

## Example Sentences
*   *Je parle français.* (I speak French.)
*   *Nous mangeons un croissant.* (We are eating a croissant.)
*   *Elles travaillent à Paris.* (They work in Paris.)
