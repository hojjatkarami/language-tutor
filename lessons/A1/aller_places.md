# The Verb Aller & Prepositions of Place (A1)

The verb **aller** (to go) is essential for describing destination or state of health. It is irregular in the present tense.

## Conjugation of Aller (Present Tense)
| Pronoun | Conjugation | English |
|---|---|---|
| Je | **vais** | I go / am going |
| Tu | **vas** | You go / are going |
| Il / Elle / On | **va** | He / She / One goes |
| Nous | **allons** | We go / are going |
| Vous | **allez** | You go / are going |
| Ils / Elles | **vont** | They go / are going |

## Prepositions of Place with 'à'
When going to a place, the preposition **à** combines with definite articles:
*   **à + le** = **au** (masculine singular): *Je vais au cinéma.*
*   **à + la** = **à la** (feminine singular): *Je vais à la plage.*
*   **à + l'** = **à l'** (before vowel/silent h): *Je vais à l'école.*
*   **à + les** = **aux** (plural): *Je vais aux toilettes.*

## Using 'chez'
For going to a person's home or professional office, use **chez**:
*   *Je vais chez le médecin.* (I am going to the doctor's.)
*   *Je vais chez mon ami.* (I am going to my friend's house.)
