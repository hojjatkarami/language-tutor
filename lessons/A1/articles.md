# Definite and Indefinite Articles (A1)

In French, all nouns have a gender (masculine or feminine) and a number (singular or plural). The articles matching these nouns must agree in gender and number.

## Definite Articles (The)
Definite articles refer to a specific, defined object or person.

| Gender/Number | Article | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Masculine Singular** | **Le** (or **L'** before vowel/h) | le livre / l'ami | the book / the friend |
| **Feminine Singular** | **La** (or **L'** before vowel/h) | la table / l'école | the table / the school |
| **Plural (both genders)** | **Les** | les livres / les écoles | the books / the schools |

## Indefinite Articles (A / An / Some)
Indefinite articles refer to non-specific items.

| Gender/Number | Article | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Masculine Singular** | **Un** | un chat | a cat |
| **Feminine Singular** | **Une** | une pomme | an apple |
| **Plural (both genders)** | **Des** | des chats / des pommes | (some) cats / (some) apples |

## Example Sentences
*   *J'aime **le** café.* (I like the coffee - general preference uses definite article).
*   *C'est **un** ami de Pierre.* (He is a friend of Pierre).
*   *Elle a acheté **des** pommes.* (She bought some apples).
