# The Present Conditional (Le Conditionnel Présent) (B1)

The **conditionnel présent** is used to express wishes, hypotheses, polite requests, or advice. It translates to "would + verb".

## How to Form the Conditional
> **Stem = Future Stem** + **Endings = Imparfait Endings**

The endings are:
*   Je: **-ais**
*   Tu: **-ais**
*   Il/Elle/On: **-ait**
*   Nous: **-ions**
*   Vous: **-iez**
*   Ils/Elles: **-aient**

### Examples:
*   *parler* $\rightarrow$ Future stem: *parler-* $\rightarrow$ *Je parler**ais*** (I would speak)
*   *être* $\rightarrow$ Future stem: *ser-* $\rightarrow$ *Nous ser**ions*** (We would be)
*   *avoir* $\rightarrow$ Future stem: *aur-* $\rightarrow$ *Ils aur**aient*** (They would have)

## Key Use Cases of the Present Conditional

### 1. Polite Requests or Suggestions (*La Politesse*)
Softens statements to make them polite compared to using the direct present tense.
*   *Pourriez-vous m'aider ?* (Could you help me?)
*   *Je voudrais un verre d'eau, s'il vous plaît.* (I would like a glass of water, please.)

### 2. Giving Advice or Recommendations (*Le Conseil*)
Gently recommends actions or options to someone.
*   *Tu devrais dormir plus.* (You should sleep more.)
*   *On pourrait aller au cinéma ce soir.* (We could go to the movies tonight.)

### 3. Wishes and Desires (*Le Souhait / Le Désir*)
Expresses dreams, wishes, or strong preferences.
*   *J'aimerais voyager en Suisse.* (I would love to travel to Switzerland.)
*   *Nous préférerions partir tôt.* (We would prefer to leave early.)

### 4. Hypothetical Situations (*L'Hypothèse*)
Used in "if-then" clauses that depend on a condition.
> **Si + Imparfait $\rightarrow$ Conditionnel Présent**
*   *Si j'avais le temps, je ferais du sport.* (If I had time, I would do sports.)
*   *Si tu étudiais, tu réussirais.* (If you studied, you would succeed.)

### 5. Future in the Past (*Le Futur dans le passé*)
Reports a future event from a past perspective (often in indirect speech).
*   *Il a dit qu'il viendrait.* (He said that he would come.)

### 6. Unconfirmed Information (*L'Information non confirmée*)
Common in journalism to state rumors or unverified facts.
*   *Selon la police, le suspect habiterait encore à Paris.* (According to police, the suspect is believed to still live in Paris.)
