# Stylistic Inversion & Formal Structures (B2)

In written and formal spoken French, subject-verb inversion is used not only for questions but also for style and emphasis, particularly after certain introductory adverbs.

## 1. Inversion after specific adverbs
When a sentence begins with **peut-être** (perhaps), **à peine** (scarcely/barely), or **sans doute** (probably), formal French inverts the subject and verb:
*   *Peut-être qu'il viendra.* (Standard) $\rightarrow$ *Peut-être **viendra-t-il**.* (Formal)
*   *A peine il avait fini...* (Standard) $\rightarrow$ *A peine **avait-il** fini...* (Formal)

## 2. Inversion in relative clauses
In relative clauses, the subject can be placed after the verb for a smoother sentence rhythm, especially when the subject is long:
*   *La maison que **mon grand-père a construite**.* (Standard) $\rightarrow$ *La maison qu'**a construite mon grand-père**.* (Formal/Stylistic)

## 3. The euphonic -t- in inversion
When inverting, if the verb ends in a vowel and the pronoun starts with a vowel (il/elle/on), insert a **-t-** between hyphens to ease pronunciation:
*   *Il parle.* $\rightarrow$ *Parle-**t**-il ?*
*   *Elle a.* $\rightarrow$ *A-**t**-elle ?*
