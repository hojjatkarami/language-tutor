# Present Participle vs. Gerund (B2)

In French, both the **participe présent** (e.g., *parlant*) and the **gérondif** (e.g., *en parlant*) end in **-ant**, but they have different grammatical functions.

## 1. The Gerund (Le Gérondif)
Always preceded by **en**. It denotes **simultaneity** (while doing) or **manner/cause** (by doing). The subject of both clauses must be the same:
*   *Elle marche **en écoutant** de la musique.* (She walks while listening to music.)

## 2. The Present Participle (Le Participe Présent)
Used without *en*. It can have different subjects and functions as:
*   **A relative clause replacement**:
    *   *Les étudiants **souhaitant** s'inscrire...* (The students **wishing** to register... = *qui souhaitent*)
*   **A cause indicator** (equivalent to "since" or "because"):
    *   *N'**ayant** pas d'argent, il est resté chez lui.* (Having no money, he stayed home.)
